Kalendar

Privacy policy

Last updated: 2 November 2026

Short version: Kalendar runs on your own PC. There is no Kalendar server, no account, no tracking and no ads. Your calendar stays with you.

What Kalendar stores

Your events, settings, pictures and team logos are saved on your PC, in Kalendar's own folder (%APPDATA%\Kalendar). They are never sent to the developer. Uninstalling Kalendar and deleting that folder removes everything.

Google Calendar

If you choose to connect a Google account, Kalendar asks Google for permission to see and edit your calendars, and for your email address (only to show which account is connected).

  • Kalendar uses this access only to keep the calendars you choose in step with Kalendar on your PC: it reads your events to show them in Kalendar, and writes the events you create, change or delete in Kalendar back to Google. It also sends your answer when you accept or decline an invite.
  • Calendar data goes directly between your PC and Google. It is not sent to the developer or anyone else, not sold, not used for advertising, and not used to train AI models.
  • Your Google sign-in is stored encrypted on your PC. Disconnect in Kalendar's settings deletes it and tells Google to revoke it. You can also remove Kalendar's access at any time at myaccount.google.com/permissions.

Kalendar's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Other services Kalendar talks to

Some features look things up on the internet. These requests go straight from your PC to the service and include only what's needed for the lookup:

  • TVmaze – TV show and episode details, when you follow a show.
  • TMDB – movie details, with your own TMDB key if you add one.
  • IGDB and Twitch – game details, with your own Twitch keys if you add them.
  • MET Norway (Yr) – the weather forecast, using the coordinates of the place you choose, if you turn weather on.
  • TheSportsDB – team logos, when you search for them.
  • Photon (OpenStreetMap) – place suggestions while you type a place.
  • Calendar links you subscribe to, such as a FotMob fixture list.
  • Pictures you add by link – the website you paste an address from, once, when you add the picture. Kalendar keeps its own copy on your PC.
  • GitHub – checking for new versions of Kalendar.

Each of these services has its own privacy policy.
